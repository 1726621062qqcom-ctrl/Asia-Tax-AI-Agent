12: Conduit countries and treaty shopping
Alfons J. Weichenrieder
and
Dmitry Erokhin https://orcid.org/0000-0002-5191-0579
in Research Handbook On The Economics Of Tax Havens
Category:
Chapter
Published:
05 Dec 2024
Page Range:
218–237
Collection:
Economics 2024
DOI:
https://doi.org/10.4337/9781803929743.00021
Publisher:
Edward Elgar Publishing
Keywords:
Conduit entities; Indirect investment; Withholding tax; Double taxation; Treaty shopping
  • Abstract
  • References

Foreign direct investment may involve indirect ownership through third-country (conduit) entities. This structure is often driven by varying dividend withholding tax rates depending on the investor's home country. Such differentiated tax rates can lead to treaty shopping strategies. This chapter examines the empirical evidence on treaty shopping, including the possible overestimation of foreign direct investment and potential tax revenue losses. While there is a focus on the negative aspects of treaty shopping in the literature, it is important to note that it can also reduce economic double taxation, offering potential benefits. In some cases, indirect ownership structures may also be motivated by the avoidance of wealth taxes or capital gains taxes; intransparent tax havens may be used in conduit structures to obscure the true origin of the ultimate investor.
